The C2PA conformance program for generator products helps you
demonstrate that your product implements Content Credentials correctly
when signing assets. It verifies that your product conforms to
the specification and that your implementation meets certain security
requirements.

Conformance helps the ecosystem function by improving interoperability
between products, giving consumers confidence that Content Credentials
can be trusted, and encouraging alignment around the current 2.x specification
series rather than fragmenting across older versions.

:::note
This guide was written for the 0.2 version of the C2PA conformance program, released in mid-2026.  We will make every effort to keep this guide up-to-date with subsequent changes to the program, but always confirm information with the [C2PA conformance page](https://c2pa.org/conformance/).
:::

This guide assumes your implementation is complete and ready for
evaluation. It walks through the conformance application process, from
submitting your expression of interest to receiving a notice of conformance
that you can use to obtain a claim-signing certificate from a certificate
authority (CA).

If you're still building your implementation or need help getting
Content Credentials working in your product, start with these resources:

- [CAI SDK docs](https://opensource.contentauthenticity.org/docs/getting-started)
- [CAI video tutorials](https://learn.contentauthenticity.org)
- [CAI Discord server](https://discord.gg/CAI)

## Types of applicants

The C2PA conformance program covers a range of applicants, depending on the product type, and (for generator products) implementation class and maximum assurance level.

### Product types

C2PA recognizes three broad product types. You must apply separately for each type of product:

- **Generator products** attach Content Credentials to assets using a claim-signing certificate issued under a root on the C2PA Trust List.
- **Validator products** read and validate Content Credentials attached by C2PA-conforming generator products.
- **Certificate authorities** issue C2PA claim-signing certificates to generator products from their root on the C2PA Trust List.

### Implementation classes

Generator products have three implementation classes, depending on where the product performs the critical functions of assertion management, manifest construction, and claim signing:

- **Edge**: Critical functions run on an endpoint such as a phone,
  camera, or laptop.
- **Backend**: Critical functions run entirely on the server.
- **Distributed**: Critical functions are split between edge and backend
  subsystems.

### Assurance levels

Each application for generator product conformance specifies a *maximum assurance
level* that the product seeks. The assurance level tells relying parties
how much confidence they can place in the claims it signs. The program
currently defines two levels:

- **Assurance level 1**: The generator product follows documented security practices, including secure authentication when requesting certificates, an encrypted signing key with controlled access, scanning for known vulnerabilities with timely patching, and protected traffic and hosting.
- **Assurance level 2**: The generator product meets level 1 criteria and additionally holds its signing key in secure hardware-backed storage such as a cloud key management service or device keystore. When requesting certificates, it must also present hardware-verified dynamic evidence of its identity, key possession, and current security updates.

:::note
These descriptions are summaries. The authoritative requirements are in the [C2PA Generator Product Security Requirements](https://github.com/c2pa-org/conformance-public/blob/main/docs/v0.2/C2PA%20Generator%20Product%20Security%20Requirements.pdf) document.
:::

### Example app

This guide walks through the conformance process using an
open-source application called [Conformance Example App](https://github.com/contentauth/conformance-example-app)
applying as a **generator product** with a **backend** implementation
class at **assurance level 1**.

This type of implementation uses on-premises servers or a commercial
cloud. The example application generates its assertions, manifests,
claims, and claim signatures on the server. It's a limited example that
works only with JPEG files. It validates the files (signed or unsigned)
as ingredients, edits them, and then outputs signed JPEG files.

:::tip
The walkthrough shows the process as if you had developed the [example
app](https://github.com/contentauth/conformance-example-app)
and were applying for conformance for it. Examples of the information you
would enter or select are shown in *italics*. For your own application,
enter information specific to you and your product.
:::

## 2. Legal agreements

Each product type for which you apply requires its own legal
agreement signed by an authorized officer of the company.

If you apply as both a generator product and a validator product, you
must sign separate agreements for each. C2PA has carefully developed and
reviewed these agreements and expects applicants to accept them without
modification.

After C2PA receives your expression of interest form, it will ask you
to use the Linux Foundation's document signing service to execute the
legal agreement. If you're not the authorized signatory for the
company, forward the agreement to them through the signing platform.

Once you've signed the agreement, the program administrator will send an
email directing you to complete the next step in the process: the C2PA
conformance program intake form.

## 5. Security requirements diagram

Your security architecture document should include a diagram of your TOE
in section C.1.6. It gives the conformance evaluator a clear picture of your
system at a glance. Although not required, you can
follow the convention in Appendix A of the Security Requirements
document. Use a blue dashed border for the target of evaluation and a
pink dashed border for the conforming product inside it.

The target of evaluation boundary contains everything your product
depends on to produce signed assets. The conforming product boundary
contains only the components that generate assertions, construct
manifests, and request claim signing. For conformance-example-app,
that's the upload endpoint, crop engine, and signing service. The
KMS and storage are trusted infrastructure inside the TOE but outside
the pink boundary, and the browser sits outside both with an arrow
pointing in.

## 6. Sample evidence

Once you submit your application materials, the conformance
administrator will request *sample evidence*. You'll provide a signed
output file for every media type you listed as generated or validated on
your intake form, along with the crJSON or JSON manifest report for
each one.

If your product validates specific media types as ingredients, your
samples must show that ingestion for each one. The conformance program
maintains a library of conformant sample assets you can use as
ingredients. Your manifest reports should show that each ingredient is
valid and its signing credential is trusted, so configure the C2PA Trust
List and C2PA TSA Trust List before validating. If the library doesn't
include a conformant asset for a media type you need, you can sign an
asset of that type with a test certificate to demonstrate validation for
that media type.

Use these samples to demonstrate the interoperability testing you
asserted on the intake form.

The Conformance Example App opens one of the library's conformant JPEGs,
adds it as an ingredient, crops it, and signs a new manifest recording
the crop action and the ingredient. Submit the signed JPEG with its
crJSON manifest report.

For example, the Conformance Example App configures trust in [`manifestManager.js`](https://github.com/contentauth/conformance-example-app/blob/c87eaddce266eb0dd0eade7a30b7919a254c940c/functions/manifestManager.js#L21).

The Conformance Example App then opens the existing file and adds it as an ingredient [later in `manifestManager.js`](https://github.com/contentauth/conformance-example-app/blob/c87eaddce266eb0dd0eade7a30b7919a254c940c/functions/manifestManager.js#L68).

## 7. Assessment and approval

### Assessment

The administrator reviews everything you've submitted against the
program requirements, including your intake form, security architecture
document and diagram, and sample evidence. Expect questions by email
during this step.

The administrator returns a conformance review report that walks through
your security architecture document objective by objective. For each
requirement at your target level, the report gives a verdict of "meets,"
"does not meet," or "not applicable," with reviewer notes explaining the
finding. It closes with an overall verdict and a list of required
remediation steps. If you receive a "Not Conformant" verdict, address
each remediation step, update your document or implementation, and
resubmit.

If you need implementation help during this phase, ask in
the **#c2pa-conformance** channel on the [CAI Discord server](https://discord.gg/CAI).

Once the administrator is satisfied, your record goes to an approver.

### Approval

The program requires at least two people to sign off before final
acceptance and approval. After the administrator recommends your
product, a separate approver reviews your record and supporting
evidence. If the approver finds non-conformities or has questions,
they'll send them to you for answers or remediation.

When both the administrator and the approver are satisfied, your final
record is ready for publication.

## 8. Notice of conformance

After approval, the administrator sends you a digitally signed *Notice
of Conformant Status* letter. The letter confirms your product is
conformant at your maximum assurance level and includes your product's
record for the conforming products list. The record contains a record
ID, your legal organization name, your product's distinguished name (DN)
values, minimum software version, assurance level, supported specification
versions, the media
types you generate and validate, and relevant dates.

You can then provide this letter to a CA on the C2PA Trust List to obtain your
production claim-signing certificate.

## 9. Public record posting

Before publishing, the administrator previews your record and confirms
its contents. C2PA then adds your record to the [C2PA Conforming
Products List](https://github.com/c2pa-org/conformance-public/blob/main/conforming-products/conforming-products-list.json),
typically within 72 hours of the notice, or on your earliest public
disclosure date if you set one in your application. You can view
published records using the [Conformance Explorer](https://spec.c2pa.org/conformance-explorer/).

Your record covers the product as submitted. If you make a material
change, meaning a change to anything in your record or to how your
product meets the security requirements, you must reapply and receive a
new record ID.
